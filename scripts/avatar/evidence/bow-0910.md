# The goodbye bowed backwards on every body the site offers

2026-09-10. `bye` played a gesture that leaned Mika AWAY from the visitor. It
reads as a bow on screen because her head drops at the same time, which is why
it survived from the first day the cue existed. This is what it actually did,
what the corrected gesture does, and how both were measured.

## 1. Why one curve means two things

`avatarGuideEngine`'s gesture table writes head and spine pitch as plain
numbers, and a normalized bone's local axes follow the MODEL's. The two VRM
versions face opposite ways along Z, so the same positive pitch carries her head
toward the model's +Z either way and that is FORWARD on a 1.0 body and BACKWARD
on a 0.x one. `VRMUtils.rotateVRM0` turns a 0.x body round so she faces the
camera, and does not touch this: it rotates the scene above the rig.

Every look this site offers -- `pink`, `milfy`, `base` -- is the same VRoid 0.x
family. The two 1.0 bodies in the registry are `offered: false`, so the version
that got the sign right was the one no visitor could see.

This is the same shape as the rest pose that put a 1.0 body's arms straight up
(`armrest-0909.md`), and the fix is the same shape too: the sign belongs to the
version, so it is read from the version rather than written as a literal.
`avatarMode.facingSign` is that reader, derived from `armRestSign` rather than
stated again, because the two are one fact with opposite signs.

## 2. What each gesture does, measured

Offline first, on each registered family's own body, applying `bow`'s own
offsets to a real skeleton (`pitch-0909.log`, `pitch-0909-probe.ts`; both were
written on 09-09, so the id `bow` in them is the curve now called `leanBack`):

| body | VRM | the old curve carried her head |
|---|---|---|
| `vroid-sample-b` (every offered look) | 0 | **121.8mm AWAY from her face** |
| `vrm1-twist-sample` | 1 | 136.1mm toward it |
| `vroid-studio-dressup` | 1 | 136.1mm toward it |

Then in the browser, through the real engine and the real gesture button, on the
milfy body the site serves. Eye world position at the envelope peak, against a
baseline averaged over a full breath:

| gesture | eye Z, three passes | reads as |
|---|---:|---|
| `bow` (corrected) | **+137.5, +145.5, +151.1mm toward the viewer** | a bow |
| `leanBack` (the old curve) | **-152.9, -147.3, -137.8mm away** | a lean back |

About 14mm is what this method repeats to across those three, so the prose says
"about 145mm each way" (the three-pass mean is 144.7mm forward, 146.0mm back)
rather than carrying a tenth of a millimetre that does not survive another run.
The idle baseline itself moved 14.5mm between two consecutive runs, which is the
same floor. The SIGN is what carries the argument, and it is the same in all
three passes and in the offline probe. Every pass, every gesture and the method
are in `bow-0910-browser.log`; it exists because the page is a dev-server
session and nothing else records them.

**Which way is toward the viewer** is read off the camera, not inferred from
another gesture. `avatarGuideEngine` puts it at `camera.position.set(0, y +
AVATAR_CAMERA_TILT, framingDistance)` with `AVATAR_FRAMING_DEFAULT.distance =
2.3`, so the camera sits at positive Z and +Z is toward the person watching.
`VRMUtils.rotateVRM0` sets `scene.rotation.y = Math.PI` on a 0.x body, which is
what turns a model that faces -Z round to face that camera, and is also why it
cannot fix the pitch: it rotates the scene above the rig, and the gesture writes
inside it.

An earlier draft argued this from `nod` instead, and the arithmetic does not
support that: `nod`'s pitch peaks at 0.14 rad, which at the 720ms sample is
0.078 rad and about 4.5mm of eye swing, against the 10.9mm recorded. That is
inside the same band the eight untouched gestures sit in, so it was noise being
read as an anchor. The conclusion did not move; the reason it rests on did.

`bow-0910-bow.png` and `bow-0910-leanback.png` are those two poses on screen: in
one she is tipped toward the camera with her hair swung forward and her face
foreshortened, in the other her chin is up, her whole face reads, and her hair
hangs behind her shoulders. Each was captured from inside the page on the very
frame its number above was read on, and that frame is the largest |Z| of the
whole 1.5s trace rather than a nominal 720ms.

The first pair of PNGs committed under these names were not these poses, and
both reviewers caught it. They were two near-idle frames: 172,428 against
172,427 foreground pixels and a silhouette centroid 0.6px apart, the whole
difference a 4px sideways idle sway. The cause is that an outside screenshot
tool cannot be aimed at a 1.5s envelope, because nothing bounds its latency. The
replacements differ the way two poses do: 571,922 against 561,288 foreground
pixels, topmost foreground row 9px apart, foreground centroid 66.6px apart in
Y. The method, the guard against a blank capture, how foreground is defined,
and every silhouette measurement are in `bow-0910-browser.log`.

**Averaging the baseline is load-bearing.** Two earlier passes read the peak
against a single instant of the idle pose and returned OPPOSITE signs on
consecutive runs: breathing and weight shift move her eyes by the same tens of
millimetres the gesture does in Y. Only the Z swing is far enough above that
floor to carry an argument: the eight other gestures in the same pass all read
under 11mm in Z except the head turn, against 137.5 and -152.9 for the two bows.
Nothing in this note rests on a Y reading.

## 3. What changed

* `bow` is now the version-aware curve, and it is what `bye` plays.
* `leanBack` is the old curve under the name of what it does, byte-identical in
  behaviour. It kept a place in the table because it is a usable pose and
  because deleting a motion that already shipped is not what was asked for.
* The preview page offers both, labelled 鞠躬 and 後仰.

The other eight gestures were watched in the same browser pass and none moved
more than the idle floor except `glance`, which is a head turn and reads +92.9mm
because that is what a head turn does. That says `bow` changing did not disturb
them; it does not say they were right. Three of them carried the same defect and
were fixed the same afternoon, in `pitch-0910.md`, and so did the mode-driven
gaze they all ride on, in `aim-0910.md`.

## 4. Mutations

Seven, in `bow-0910-mutations.log`, every one red. They land on five distinct
tests: G1 and G2 each redden both bow assertions, and G6 and G7 exist to redden
one of that pair each, which is how the head half and the spine half are shown
to be separately covered.

| mutation | what goes red |
|---|---|
| `facingSign` returns one sign for both versions | both bow assertions |
| `bow` ignores which way the body faces | both |
| the SPINE half alone ignores it | `bow carries her head toward the viewer` |
| the HEAD half alone ignores it | `bow tips her face toward the viewer too` |
| `leanBack`'s curve is edited at all | `leaves leanBack pitching toward the model own +Z` (all three families) |
| the goodbye goes back to the old curve | `bows toward the visitor when the chat is stowed` |
| the loop writes a fixed sign instead of reading the body | `hands the gesture table THIS body version too`, renamed by aim-0910.md to `reads the facing off the body every frame, and gives it to the gestures` |

Three of the seven owe their redness to a reviewer reading the first draft of
this note: G6 and G7 by existing at all, and G5 by turning red.

The head half of the fix was not covered at all. The first version of
`avatarBow.test.ts` read the HEAD bone's own world position, and rotating a bone
does not move its own origin, so every millimetre it reported came from `sx` on
the spine: dropping the version term from `hp` alone left the whole suite green.
The head term now has an assertion that reads the EYE, which sits about 57mm
above the head pivot and does swing, and the two halves are mutated separately.

The loop's version read was recorded as an unclosable gap, on the grounds that
the line reading the body's version lives inside the render loop's closure and
needs a real VRM and a WebGL context. That was wrong, and a reviewer found it.
`pinArms` has the same seam and does NOT have it open:
`avatarGuideEngine.wiring.test.ts` reads the engine's source and asserts that
pinArms takes `v.meta.metaVersion`, deliberately dumb, no VRM and no WebGL: the
whole file reads 2ms to 9ms over the nineteen runs in `bow-0910-timing.log`. The
gesture loop now has the same kind of assertion beside it, since aim-0910.md
scoped to the render loop's own body and split by what each one claims. What
that kind of test cannot say is that the bow looks right; the browser pass in
section 2 is what says that.
