# She raised her chin to look at the floor, for as long as anyone was typing

2026-09-10, the last of the version-mirror class. `bow` went first because it
was the visible one (`bow-0910.md`), then the three gestures that play without
being asked (`pitch-0910.md`). Both of those are beats: they last a second or
two and then let go. This one is the layer underneath, and it never lets go. It
is what her head is doing in every frame a visitor sees.

## 1. One pitch, two consumers, one of which mirrors

`headAim(mode, t)` returns a single `pitch`, and the engine spends it twice.
As it stood before this change:

```
head.rotation.x = pitch * 0.7      neck.rotation.x = pitch * 0.3
    the MODEL's own space, so it means opposite things on the two versions
eyeTarget.position.y = 1.35 + Math.sin(pitch) * -4
    WORLD space, so a positive pitch sends her gaze down on every body
```

The second line is unchanged today and is pinned as it stands; the first two now
read `fwd * pitch * 0.7` and `fwd * pitch * 0.3`, off `aimPitchPose`.

The eye target is what defines the sign. `pitch` means "look down" because that
is what it does to the target, on any body, and `VRMUtils.rotateVRM0` does not
touch it: the target is added to the root scene, a sibling of the `loaded.scene`
that gets turned. The bones have to agree with it, and on a 0.x body they did
the opposite.

Every look this site offers is the same VRoid 0.x family, so the wrong direction
is the one every visitor got. `listening` is the mode with by far the largest
pitch range of the three, -0.20 to +0.12 against idle's -0.05 to +0.05, and it
is the mode a visitor holds her in while they type.

The engine does not spend those numbers raw. `headAim`'s output goes through
`stepHeadAim`, a one-pole ease that attenuates the 1.6s `listening` term to
0.8496 at 60fps, so the pitch reaching the bones is -0.04 +/- 0.136 and the
deepest look-down arrives as +0.096. The head takes 0.7 of that: **3.85 degrees**
of chin the wrong way, held for as long as the visitor keeps typing, and 7.06
degrees the wrong way at the deepest look-up. The attenuation is derived and the
angles are then read off a measurement rather than off the derivation: the
browser pass reads the neck bone at -0.0288 and +0.0528, the head carries 7/3 of
the neck, and 0.0288 * 7 / 3 is 0.0672 rad. An earlier draft of this file quoted
4.81 degrees, which is `0.12 * 0.7`, the angle the ease never lets the bone
reach. Both reviewers caught it.

The fix is `aimPitchPose(pitch, fwd)` in `avatarMode.ts`, the module that
already owns `facingSign` and `armRestSign` and so already owns this class.
`aimYawPose(yaw)` moved with it and carries no facing term, for the reason in
section 3.

## 2. In the browser, through the real engine

Full method, both bodies and every number in `aim-0910-browser.log`. The shape
of it: 12 seconds of `listening` on the milfy body the site serves, reading her
gaze target and her own eye every frame, with a VRM 1.0 body measured in the
same session as a control.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| eye above her head pivot, at her lowest gaze target | 59.87mm | 53.66mm |
| the same, at her highest | 50.44mm | 61.71mm |
| correlation of the two over 721 frames | -0.957 | +0.944 |

Before, her eye sat HIGHER above her head pivot the lower her gaze target went.
The 1.0 control body reads +0.958 before and +0.944 after: it does not move,
which is what says the flip is the facing term and not the session.

## 3. Which axes spoil, measured again on this layer

The gesture table's scope decision was measured on single rotations
(`pitch-0910.md` section 2), and the same three axes carry this layer, so the
same answer holds. What is new here is that this layer writes yaw on THREE bones
at once, so it gets its own reading rather than inheriting one:

| | on a 1.0 body | on a 0.x body | mirrors? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `aimYawPose(0.1)` on head, neck and spine | eye 2.16mm toward her left | eye 3.26mm toward her left | no |
| `aimPitchPose(0.12, fwd)` head term | eye 2.02mm down | eye 2.69mm down | fixed by `fwd` |
| `aimPitchPose(0.12, fwd)` neck term | eye 1.19mm down | eye 1.48mm down | fixed by `fwd` |

Yaw goes the same way on both, so `aimYawPose` has no facing term and a test on
every registered body says so. Signing it would be a new defect: on a 0.x body
`fwd` is -1, so the same call would read -3.47mm and turn her the other way.

## 4. The breathing and the weight shift, which are left alone

The layer also writes three terms that are not the gaze, and they mirror too, so
the decision to leave them is a decision and not an oversight:

```
chest.rotation.x = Math.sin(t * 2pi / 4.2) * 0.012 + OFF.cx
hips.rotation.z  = sway * 0.02          sway = Math.sin(t * 2pi / 13)
spine.rotation.z = sway * -0.012 + OFF.sz
```

Measured the same way as everything else, on all three family bodies:
`chest.rotation.x = +0.012` carries her eye 4.51mm toward her face on a 1.0 body
and 4.06mm AWAY from it on a 0.x one; `sway = +1` carries it 4.95mm toward her
right on a 1.0 body and 4.61mm toward her left on a 0.x one. Both mirror.

They are left as they are because each is a symmetric sine with zero mean whose
only consumer is the bone it is written on. Flipping the sign of such a term
gives the same set of poses at a different moment: 2.1 seconds later for the
breathing, 6.5 for the weight shift. Nothing reads the phase, so there is no
frame you could point at and say it is wrong.

That argument is exactly what the gaze does not get, and the difference is worth
naming, because it is the test for the next term anyone adds here. The gaze
pitch fails it twice over: `listening` carries a DC offset of -0.04, so its two
halves are not the same size, and it has a second consumer in world space that
does not mirror. Either one alone would be enough.

`OFF.cx` is a declared channel with no producer: no gesture in the table writes
it today. If one ever does it becomes a mirrored pitch with no facing term, and
the two version-blind tests would call it correct, because "identical at both
signs of `fwd`" is precisely what an unsigned pitch looks like. So `cx` is named
alongside `hp` and `sx` in the pitch-channel guard described below.

## 5. What holds it

Three assertions in `avatarPitch.test.ts` pose a real skeleton, on each
registered family's own body, and read where her eye ends up:

- the head term agrees with the eye target, looking down and looking up
- the neck term agrees with it, its own test because dropping the facing from
  one half leaves the other half's test green
- all three yaw terms turn her toward her own left, on either version

A fourth reads the gesture table instead of a body: every gesture either
resolves its pitch against the facing or is the one named exception,
`leanBack`, checked across all three pitch channels, `hp`, `sx` and `cx`.

Four in `avatarGuideEngine.wiring.test.ts` read the engine's source, because
none of the above can see whether the engine calls any of it. The first was
already there for the gesture table and now covers both layers:

- the facing is read off the body EVERY FRAME and handed to the gesture table
  (the gaze layer's half of the same fact is the third bullet below)
- the gaze goes through `aimPitchPose` and `aimYawPose`, with the three yaw
  ratios and the two pitch ratios absent from the engine as literals
- the pitch call is handed `fwd` rather than a literal, its own test because the
  call can be there and be given a 1
- the eye target still reads `1.35 + Math.sin(pitch) * -4`

The last of those is the one with nothing else behind it. That expression is
what makes `pitch` mean "look down"; `avatarPitch.test.ts` holds a copy of it to
compare signs against, so flipping the minus in the engine would put the bones
and the target back in disagreement with that whole file green. A reviewer found
it open and the mutation W5 is what closes it.

"every frame" is likewise a reviewer's finding. A regex over the whole file is
position-blind, and hoisting `const fwd = facingSign(...)` out of the loop to
the top of `initAvatarGuide` leaves every string those assertions match intact
while pinning `fwd` to +1 for the life of the engine, because `vrm` is null up
there and `?? '1'` fills in. So those two assertions are scoped to the frame
loop's own body, and W4 is the mutation that proves the scoping bites.

One mutation per defence, with the map of which test each reddens and the
runner's verbatim output in `aim-0910-mutations.log`. The map is not one-to-one
and that file says why for each row: W1 removes two properties in one edit, and
several of the others come at a test from the opposite side to another. The
mutations from the gesture phase are re-run in the same file, because this
change edits the one that carries their assertions.
